The three card tarot spread is the workhorse of tarot. It is short enough to read in a few minutes, yet it gives you a sense of movement that a single card cannot. The default positions are Past, Present, and Future, but the real strength of three cards is that the labels can be changed to match almost any question. This guide covers the main variations and, most importantly, how to read the three cards as one message rather than three separate ones. All of this is for reflection and entertainment only.

## The default layout: Past, Present, Future

Lay the cards left to right.

- **Past** shows what led here: a decision, a mood, an event, or a habit still shaping the situation.
- **Present** shows where things stand now, including the part you may not be seeing clearly.
- **Future** shows the likely direction if nothing changes. It is a trajectory, not a fixed destination.

This layout suits questions like "Where is this relationship heading?", "What is the arc of my current job?", or "How is my relationship with money evolving?"

## Variation: Situation, Action, Outcome

Use this when you need to decide something.

- **Situation** describes the circumstances as they really are.
- **Action** suggests the stance or step that fits those circumstances.
- **Outcome** shows what that action tends to lead to.

This is the best three card layout for [decision questions](/questions/decision). It replaces the passive "what will happen" with the more useful "what happens if I do this."

## Variation: You, Them, Relationship

Use this for any question involving two people: partners, friends, colleagues, family.

- **You** reflects your own position, feelings, or contribution.
- **Them** reflects the other person's position as far as the cards can suggest it.
- **Relationship** shows the dynamic between you, which is often different from either side alone.

Be careful with the "Them" card. It is a prompt for empathy, not a window into someone's private mind. Read it as "what might be true for them" and hold it lightly. See the [love reading guide](/questions/love) for more.

## Other useful trios

- **Mind, Body, Spirit** for a personal check-in.
- **Option A, Option B, What I am missing** for an either/or choice.
- **What helps, What hinders, What to do** for a stuck project.

The rule is simple: choose labels before you draw, write them down, and do not change them after you see the cards.

## How to read three cards together

Reading three cards well is mostly about resisting the urge to read them in isolation.

### 1. Read each card alone

Note the card, its orientation, and its core meaning in its position. Keep each note to one line. This gives you raw material without committing to a story yet.

### 2. Look at the suits and arcana

Count the Major Arcana. Several Majors suggest the question touches something significant or long-running. Mostly Minors suggests everyday matters you can influence directly. Then look at suits: Cups point to emotion, Swords to thought and conflict, Wands to energy and ambition, Pentacles to practical and material concerns.

### 3. Find the direction of movement

In a Past/Present/Future layout, ask whether the energy is building, steady, or fading from left to right. Five of Pentacles, then Six, then Ten reads as recovery and growth. The same cards in reverse order read as something being spent down.

### 4. Notice the odd card out

If two cards agree and one does not, the disagreeing card is usually the point of the reading. Two gentle Cups cards and a reversed Tower in the Future say a quiet situation has a disruption waiting in it, and the reversal suggests you already sense it.

### 5. Write one sentence

Finish by writing a single sentence that connects all three cards. If you cannot, you have not finished reading. It does not need to be elegant; it needs to be honest.

## Worked example

Question: "What is going on with my career right now?" Layout: Past, Present, Future.

- **Past: Eight of Pentacles upright.** Steady, careful work. You built skill through repetition.
- **Present: The Wheel of Fortune reversed.** A turn that feels like bad luck or stalled momentum. Something outside your control shifted.
- **Future: The Star upright.** Renewal and a clearer sense of direction after a low point.

One sentence: the skills you built are intact, the current stall is a cycle rather than a verdict, and the direction ahead is quieter and more hopeful than it feels today.

## Common mistakes

- **Redrawing the Future card** because it is uncomfortable. The discomfort is the information.
- **Treating every reversal as negative.** A reversed card is a nuance on the same theme.
- **Asking three questions at once.** Three cards answer one question from three angles.

The three card spread rewards practice. Draw it for small questions often, and it becomes a reliable way to think through anything.
