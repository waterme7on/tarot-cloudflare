A decision tarot reading is the one most people actually want: "Should I do this or not?" Tarot can help, but not by handing down a verdict. It helps by showing you the shape of the choice, the part you have been avoiding, and the way you are leaning before you admit it. This guide covers how to frame yes/no and either/or questions, how to read a card for a lean, and how to follow through. Decision readings are for reflection and entertainment only. For anything with real stakes, the cards are one input among several.

## Two kinds of decision question

**Yes/no.** "Should I apply for this role?" "Is now the time to have the conversation?" These work with a [single card](/spreads/one-card) and a rule for reading the lean.

**Either/or.** "Stay in this city or move?" "Option A or Option B?" These work with a [three card spread](/spreads/three-card) using Option A / Option B / What I am missing, or Situation / Action / Outcome.

For a decision with history and several people involved, the [Celtic Cross](/spreads/celtic-cross) shows the wider context.

## Good and bad decision questions

**Questions that work:**

- "What do I need to see before I decide about the move?"
- "What is the cost of saying yes that I am underestimating?"
- "What attitude would help me choose between these two offers?"
- "Should I bring this up this week, or wait?" (with a yes/no rule set in advance)

**Questions that do not work:**

- "Which choice will make me happy?" Too large; the cards cannot audit your future happiness.
- "Should I leave my partner?" A single card cannot carry a choice that size. Ask what you need to understand before deciding.
- "Tell me what to do." The cards are prompts, not authorities.

## How to phrase a decision question

1. **State the options concretely.** "Accept the agency offer" rather than "change jobs."
2. **Set the rule for yes/no before drawing.** Upright leans yes, reversed leans no, and the card's meaning gives the reason. Write the rule down so you cannot change it afterwards.
3. **Add a time frame.** "This month" or "before the deadline."
4. **Ask what you are missing, not just what to do.** The third position in an either/or spread is often where the real answer lives.

## Reading a card for a lean

With a yes/no rule in place, the card's meaning adds nuance.

- **Upright Ace or a bright Major (the Sun, the Star, the World).** A clear lean toward yes with energy behind it.
- **Upright but cautious (Temperance, the Hermit).** Yes, but slowly, or after more information.
- **Reversed with a gentle card.** A soft no, or not yet.
- **Reversed with a heavy card (the Tower, Ten of Swords, the Devil).** A firmer no, or a warning that saying yes means accepting the cost shown.

The lean is a starting point, not a command. If the card says yes and your stomach says no, that mismatch is the most useful thing the reading produced.

## Cards that often appear in decision readings

- **The Lovers.** Choice itself, and the need to decide by your values rather than convenience.
- **Two of Swords.** A stalemate, usually because you are refusing to look at something.
- **The Chariot.** Commit and drive forward. Reversed, pushing without a clear direction.
- **The Hanged Man.** Suspend the decision on purpose. Waiting is the choice.
- **Seven of Cups.** Too many options, some of them illusions.
- **Judgement.** A call to decide and accept the consequences. Reversed, self-doubt.
- **Wheel of Fortune.** Timing matters more than the choice; the moment is turning.

## A worked example

Question: "Stay in my current flat or move to the new one?" Three cards: Option A (stay) / Option B (move) / What I am missing.

- Stay: Four of Pentacles upright. Secure, familiar, a little tight.
- Move: Page of Wands upright. Fresh energy, some naivety about the effort involved.
- Missing: Five of Pentacles reversed. A fear of scarcity that is easing but still shaping the decision.

One sentence: staying is safe but constricting, moving is energizing but underestimated, and the leftover money fear is what makes the safe option look better than it is.

## How to act on a decision reading

- **Write the lean and the reason in one line.** "Leans yes because the Ace of Pentacles points to a solid foundation."
- **Check it against the facts.** Does the budget, calendar, or other person agree?
- **Set a deadline to decide.** The Hanged Man means wait, but not forever.
- **Do not redraw.** One draw per decision. If you must revisit, wait a week and ask a different question.
- **Own the choice.** "The cards told me to" is not a reason.

Used this way, a decision reading does not make the choice for you. It makes you honest about the one you are already leaning toward and shows you what you have been avoiding.
