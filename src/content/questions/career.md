Career questions suit tarot well because work is full of choices, timing, and relationships, and because most of us already have a half-formed opinion we have not admitted to ourselves. A good career tarot reading makes that opinion visible and gives you a frame to examine it. This guide explains what to ask, which cards tend to show up with weight in work readings, and how to turn a draw into something you actually do. Career readings are for reflection and entertainment only, not professional or financial guidance.

## Questions that work and questions that do not

**Good career questions** focus on your approach, your blind spots, or the shape of a situation:

- "What should I focus on in my work this month?"
- "What am I not seeing about this job offer?"
- "How can I handle the tension with my manager?"
- "What would help me decide between staying and leaving?"
- "What is the real lesson of this project that went badly?"

**Weak career questions** ask for a prediction you cannot act on:

- "Will I get promoted?"
- "Will the company lay people off?"
- "Is my colleague trying to undermine me?"

The weak questions are not forbidden. They are just unlikely to give you anything you can use. "Will I get promoted?" becomes much more useful as "What would strengthen my case for promotion?"

## How to phrase a career question

1. **Name the specific decision or situation.** "This offer from the design agency" rather than "my career."
2. **Ask about your contribution.** Work has many actors; the only one you control is you.
3. **Set a time frame.** "This quarter" or "before the review in March" keeps the reading grounded.
4. **Match the spread to the question.** A [one card draw](/spreads/one-card/) for a daily focus. A [three card Situation/Action/Outcome](/spreads/three-card/) for a choice. The [Celtic Cross](/spreads/celtic-cross/) when the situation has history, politics, and several people involved.

## Cards that often matter in career readings

- **Eight of Pentacles.** Skill built through steady practice. Reversed, repetition without growth, or perfectionism that slows you.
- **Three of Pentacles.** Collaboration and recognized craft. Reversed, misaligned expectations in a group.
- **Ten of Wands.** Overload. You have taken on too much, often voluntarily.
- **The Chariot.** Drive and direction. Reversed, momentum without a clear destination.
- **The Wheel of Fortune.** A turning point outside your control. Reversed, a stall that is part of a cycle.
- **Seven of Pentacles.** Assessment. Pause and check whether the effort is paying off.
- **Knight of Wands.** Bold moves, with a warning about impulsiveness.
- **King of Pentacles.** Stable authority, often a boss or an institution. Reversed, rigidity.
- **The Hermit.** Step back, think, or study before acting.
- **Six of Wands.** Recognition. Reversed, recognition delayed or going to someone else.

Watch the suits. A career reading heavy in Pentacles is about resources, pay, and structure. Heavy in Wands, it is about ambition and burnout. Heavy in Swords, the issue is communication, politics, or a decision that needs clearer thinking. Heavy in Cups, the question is really whether the work is satisfying.

## Reading a career spread

Suppose you ask "What should I know about this job offer?" with a three card Situation/Action/Outcome layout.

- Situation: Seven of Pentacles. You are at a natural point of assessment; the timing of the offer is not a coincidence.
- Action: The Hermit. Do not rush. Gather information, talk to people who work there, sit with it.
- Outcome: Three of Pentacles upright. If you take the time, collaboration and recognized skill are the likely direction.

The reading is not saying "take the job." It is saying "the offer deserves careful evaluation rather than a reflex either way, and the upside if it fits is good teamwork."

## How to act on a career reading

- **Convert the reading into one question to research.** If the Hermit appears, decide what you need to find out and from whom.
- **Pick one concrete step with a date.** Update the portfolio by Friday. Book the conversation with your manager this week. Draft the resignation letter but do not send it for two weeks.
- **Separate feeling from fact.** A reading can confirm that you feel stuck; it cannot tell you whether your savings cover three months of a job search. Check the facts separately.
- **Revisit after the step, not before.** Do the thing, then read again if you want a view from the other side.

## Common mistakes

- Asking the cards to make the decision for you.
- Reading a difficult card as "do not apply" rather than "here is what to prepare for."
- Ignoring the Action position because the Outcome card is more exciting.

A career reading works best as a short, honest conversation with yourself at a moment when you were about to avoid one. Ask the real question, read the cards as prompts, and then go do the practical work.
