## What Justice means

Justice sits at number eleven in the Major Arcana, the midpoint of the Fool's journey, and in the Rider-Waite deck she is one of the few figures who looks directly at you. She holds a raised sword in her right hand and a set of scales in her left, seated between two pillars with a purple veil behind her. The sword is double-edged: every decision cuts both ways, and the card asks you to accept that. The scales do not measure feelings; they measure cause and effect.

When Justice appears, the reading is about consequences, fairness, and clear seeing. It often shows up when a contract, agreement, verdict, or honest conversation is pending, or when you are weighing a choice that needs logic more than instinct. The crown on her head has a small square jewel, which many readers take as a sign of well-ordered thought. This is a card that rewards preparation and penalises wishful thinking.

Justice is also personal. It can describe your own integrity being tested, or your need to settle an account with someone from your past. Draw a card now to see whether Justice appears for your question. Everything here is written for reflection and entertainment, not as a substitute for a lawyer, accountant, or doctor.

## Upright

Upright Justice says the outcome will match what has been put in. If you have been honest, careful, and thorough, that will be recognised. If a dispute is in play, the upright card suggests a resolution that most reasonable people would call fair, even if it is not everything you hoped for. It also encourages you to take responsibility for your part in the situation rather than waiting to be judged.

Practically, this is the card of paperwork done properly, of agreements written down, and of decisions made after looking at both sides. It can indicate a mediator, a judge, an auditor, or simply a friend who tells you the truth without softening it.

## Reversed

Reversed, Justice describes imbalance. Someone may be avoiding accountability, bending the rules, or being treated unfairly because of bias. It can also show your own blind spot: a refusal to admit that a choice was yours, or a tendency to blame circumstances for an outcome you shaped. Delays in legal or administrative matters are common with this orientation, as is the sense that the goalposts keep moving.

The reversed card is not a sentence. It is a prompt to look at where the scales have tipped and to correct what is within your control. Sometimes that means apologising; sometimes it means gathering evidence and refusing to accept a lopsided deal.

## Love

In a relationship reading, Justice asks whether both people are giving and receiving in roughly equal measure. Upright, it shows a partnership with honest communication, clearly agreed expectations, and the maturity to repair mistakes. It can also appear when a couple formalises something, such as moving in, marrying, or combining households, where the terms need to be spelled out.

Reversed, Justice in love highlights a keeping of score, double standards, or a secret that has not been confessed. For singles, the upright card suggests being clear about what you want and attracting someone who respects that; reversed, it warns against repeating the same unfair pattern with a new face.

## Career

At work, Justice favours documented decisions, transparent processes, and roles where accuracy matters. Lawyers, compliance staff, auditors, HR professionals, and anyone negotiating a contract tend to find this card familiar. Upright, it suggests that a performance review, promotion case, or disciplinary matter will be handled on the merits.

Reversed, it can indicate office politics overriding fairness, a manager who plays favourites, or a situation where you feel blamed for a shared failure. The useful response is to keep records, stay factual, and avoid matching unfairness with more of it.

## Money

Justice with money is about balance sheets in the literal sense. Upright, it points to settlements, refunds, insurance decisions, tax matters, and inheritances resolving in a way that matches what was agreed. It encourages you to read the fine print and to make financial choices you could defend to a stranger.

Reversed, watch for arrangements that are unfair to you, unexpected penalties, or a deal where one party is not being straight. Review any agreement a second time before signing. This is a tarot interpretation, not financial advice, so verify specifics with a qualified professional.

## Yes or No

Justice leans **yes, conditionally**. The answer depends on whether your position is fair and your preparation is honest. If you are asking "Will I get what I deserve?", the card says yes, which is reassuring if you have acted well and uncomfortable if you have not. For questions where you are hoping luck will cover a shortcut, treat it as a no.

## What not to ask Justice

Do not ask Justice to tell you the result of a court case, tribunal, or official decision. Tarot cannot forecast a judge's ruling, and treating a card as legal advice can lead to poor choices. Likewise, avoid asking "Who is right?" when you already know you share some of the blame; the card will keep returning you to your own side of the scales. Better questions are "What do I need to take responsibility for?" and "Where is this situation out of balance?"
