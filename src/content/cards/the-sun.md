## What The Sun means

The Sun is the nineteenth Major Arcana card and the one readers are happiest to see. The Rider-Waite image shows a large sun with a human face and straight and wavy rays, shining over a naked child riding a white horse. The child holds a red banner, wears a wreath of flowers, and raises an arm in greeting. Behind a low wall grow four sunflowers, turning toward the child rather than toward the sun.

Everything in the image is open. The child has nothing to hide and no reason to; the horse has no saddle or bridle; the sun has no clouds in front of it. After the uncertainty of The Moon, The Sun is full daylight, where things are what they look like. The sunflowers facing the child are a quiet detail: the natural world recognising innocence and joy as its own source of light.

The card describes success, vitality, clarity, and happiness that does not need justification. Draw a card now to see whether The Sun appears for your question. This page is for reflection and entertainment and is not medical, legal, or financial advice.

## Upright

Upright, The Sun confirms that things are going well or about to. Plans succeed, health and energy are strong, and the truth of a situation is plain to see. It also encourages a particular attitude: open, direct, and unembarrassed about being glad. If you have been waiting for permission to enjoy something, this card is it.

The Sun is also a card of visibility. Your work may be noticed, your talents recognised, and your presence welcomed. It supports travel to warm places, time outdoors, and activities that involve children or a childlike openness. Where other cards hedge, The Sun says plainly: this is good.

## Reversed

Reversed, The Sun is still The Sun. Its light is dimmed, not extinguished. The usual reading is a temporary cloud: a low mood, a setback that feels bigger than it is, or an inability to appreciate success because you are focused on what is missing. It can also describe excessive optimism, where enthusiasm runs ahead of planning, or a reluctance to let yourself be seen.

A reversed Sun in a reading about a project or relationship suggests delay rather than failure. Ask what is blocking the light, whether that is fatigue, pessimism, or a situation you are keeping in the shade, and clear it.

## Love

In love, The Sun describes a relationship that feels easy and warm. There is affection, laughter, and a lack of games. For couples, it can signal engagements, weddings, pregnancies, or simply a bright period where you enjoy each other without effort. It also favours honesty: say what you feel, because the card promises it will be received well.

For singles, The Sun suggests a happy connection is likely and that your own cheerfulness is attractive. Reversed in love, the relationship may be going through a dull patch, or one partner is struggling to feel joyful for reasons that have little to do with the other. The warmth is still there under the cloud.

## Career

Professionally, The Sun marks achievement, promotion, successful launches, and public recognition. It is a strong card for presentations, interviews, and anything where you need to be seen clearly. It also favours work that brings genuine enjoyment and careers that involve children, outdoor settings, performance, or warm climates.

Reversed, The Sun at work describes a success that feels hollow, a delay in recognition, or a loss of enthusiasm for a role you once loved. It can also warn against overconfidence that skips the preparation. Reconnect with what made the work enjoyable in the first place.

## Money

With money, The Sun is a card of abundance and good outcomes. Upright, it suggests income is improving, investments or ventures are producing results, and you can afford to be generous. It also encourages spending on things that bring real pleasure rather than status. Reversed, it points to being unable to enjoy what you have, or to overspending in a burst of optimism. Keep a clear view of the figures even when the sun is shining. This is a tarot reading, not financial advice; consult a qualified professional for real money decisions.

## Yes or No

The Sun is a **yes**. It is the clearest affirmative card in the deck, and it stays positive when reversed, though a reversed Sun may mean the yes arrives later or needs a cloud removed first. If you are asking whether something will succeed, be happy, or turn out well, The Sun says it will.

## What not to ask The Sun

Do not ask The Sun to find the hidden problem. The card has no shadows in it, and if you are looking for what is wrong, this is not the card to interrogate; pull The Moon or a Swords card for that. Also avoid asking it to guarantee a specific outcome, such as a particular job or a particular person; The Sun promises happiness, not a predetermined route to it. Better questions are "What here deserves to be celebrated?" and "Where can I be more open?"
