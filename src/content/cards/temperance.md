## What Temperance means

Temperance is card fourteen in the Major Arcana, placed directly after Death, and it describes what healing looks like after an ending. In the Rider-Waite image, a winged angel stands with one foot on land and one in a pool of water, pouring liquid between two cups. The flow appears to defy gravity, moving at an angle rather than straight down. On the angel's chest is a triangle inside a square, and in the background a path winds toward distant mountains under a glowing crown.

The card's central idea is blending. Two things that seem opposed, such as reason and feeling, work and rest, or two people with different habits, are combined into something that works. The angel's feet tell the story: grounded and fluid at the same time. Temperance is not about avoiding extremes by being bland; it is about finding the exact proportion that makes a mixture hold.

It is also a card of patience. The alchemists who inspired the imagery knew that some processes cannot be hurried. Draw a card now to see whether Temperance appears for your question. This page is for reflection and entertainment, not medical, legal, or financial advice.

## Upright

Upright Temperance asks you to moderate your pace and your reactions. If you have been swinging between enthusiasm and exhaustion, the card suggests a middle setting that you can sustain. It often appears when someone is recovering, whether from a breakup, a burnout, or simply a hectic season, and signals that steady routines will do more good than dramatic fixes.

The card favours cooperation and the merging of different approaches. In a team, it means finding the method that uses everyone's strengths. In a personal decision, it means not choosing between two options when a combination is available. Patience, in this card, is active: you keep pouring, slowly, until the mixture is right.

## Reversed

Reversed, Temperance describes imbalance and excess. Something is being overdone, whether that is work, spending, exercise, drinking, or a relationship that has become all-consuming. It can also show a clash of ingredients that refuse to blend: two people with incompatible values, or a plan that tries to do too many things at once.

A second reading of the reversed card is impatience. You want results now and are tempted to force the process, which spoils it. If this card appears, check where your life has tipped too far in one direction and begin correcting it in small increments rather than with another extreme.

## Love

In love, Temperance is one of the better cards for long-term partnership. It shows two people who are different but complementary, who compromise without resentment, and who build affection through daily consistency rather than fireworks. If you are in a new relationship, the card suggests taking it at a measured pace and letting trust develop naturally.

Reversed in love, one person may be adapting so much that they disappear, or the relationship has become volatile, with big fights followed by big reconciliations. For singles, upright Temperance suggests you are becoming ready for a balanced relationship; reversed, it asks whether you are chasing intensity and mistaking it for connection.

## Career

Professionally, Temperance favours roles that blend skills: a designer who understands code, a manager who still does the craft, a scientist who can communicate. It also supports collaboration across departments and the quiet, consistent work that builds a reputation over years. If you are deciding between two paths, the card asks whether a hybrid exists.

Reversed, Temperance at work describes burnout from overcommitment, a team that cannot agree on a shared approach, or a project that is trying to satisfy everyone and pleasing no one. Scale back, choose fewer priorities, and set boundaries around your time.

## Money

With money, Temperance describes budgeting that is neither miserly nor reckless. Upright, it suggests you are finding, or need to find, a sustainable balance between saving and enjoying what you have. It supports diversified, moderate approaches over big bets. Reversed, it warns of overspending to compensate for stress, or of swinging between strict austerity and splurges. Even out your habits. For actual financial decisions, talk to a qualified professional; this is a tarot interpretation only.

## Yes or No

Temperance leans **yes**, but only for questions where a patient, measured approach is acceptable. If you are asking whether something will work out with time and care, the answer is yes. If you are asking whether you should push for an immediate, all-or-nothing result, the card's answer is closer to no. Pace is the deciding factor.

## What not to ask Temperance

Do not ask Temperance for permission to go to an extreme, whether that is quitting without notice, cutting someone off completely, or betting everything on one outcome. The card will keep pointing at the middle path, and asking it to endorse a drastic move only produces frustration. Also avoid asking "How fast can I get this?", because Temperance is more concerned with "How well?" Better questions are "Where am I out of balance?" and "What two things need to be combined here?"
