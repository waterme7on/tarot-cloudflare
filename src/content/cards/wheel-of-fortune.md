## What Wheel of Fortune means

Wheel of Fortune is card ten. The Rider-Waite image is the busiest in the Major Arcana: a large wheel floats in the sky inscribed with the letters T-A-R-O and Hebrew letters of the divine name. A sphinx with a sword sits on top. A snake descends on the left and the jackal-headed Anubis rises on the right. In the four corners, on clouds, sit a winged man, an eagle, a lion, and a bull, each reading a book.

The card is about cycles and the moments when they turn. Draw a card now to see whether Wheel of Fortune appears for your question. Everything here is for reflection and entertainment and is not medical, legal, or financial advice.

## Upright

Upright, the Wheel says a change is coming and it is in your favor. The figure rising on the right, Anubis, is the one you should identify with. Something has been stuck and is about to move. This is the card of lucky breaks, chance meetings, a door opening that you did not knock on. It is also a card of destiny in the plain sense: events larger than your planning are in motion and you are being carried.

The four creatures in the corners are the fixed signs of the zodiac, and they are reading. The lesson is that the wheel turns regardless, and the wise response is to learn from each turn rather than cling to one position. Also notice that the sphinx on top holds a sword and stays still while the wheel spins. There is a place of stability at the center of change.

## Reversed

Reversed, the wheel is turning the wrong way, or it has stopped. The first reading is a run of bad luck: delays, reversals, plans undone by circumstances. The snake descending is now the figure you are on. The card does not blame you for this; it describes a phase, and the only sure thing about a phase is that it ends.

The second reading is a cycle repeating. You are back where you were, facing the same problem with a different name, because the lesson from the last turn did not take. Reversed Wheel can also describe resisting change that is coming anyway, which uses energy and alters nothing. Bad luck wants patience. A repeating cycle wants a different choice this time around.

## Love

In love, Wheel of Fortune is the chance encounter. Upright, for singles, it suggests meeting someone through coincidence rather than effort, often while doing something unrelated. For couples it describes a shift in the relationship's phase: a new chapter, a change in circumstances that affects both of you, or a renewal after a flat period.

The card is less reassuring about permanence. The wheel turns, and a relationship that depends on luck staying good is fragile. Reversed, timing is off: the right person at the wrong moment, a pattern of relationships that end the same way, or an external event that disrupts a couple.

## Career

Career readings with the Wheel upright are good news. A turn of events brings an opportunity: a restructuring that works out in your favor, a contact who reappears, a market shift that benefits what you do. It is the card for being in the right place at the right time.

It also describes industries that run on cycles: seasonal work, markets, agriculture, anything with booms and slumps. Upright, the upswing is here. Reversed, the downturn is, or a career has stalled in a loop of similar jobs that go nowhere.

## Money

Wheel of Fortune is the card most often asked about money and the one that should be read most carefully. Upright, it indicates an improvement in circumstances: a windfall, a turn in income, a good period. It is not permission to gamble. The card's own imagery says the wheel comes back down.

Reversed, finances dip, or a cycle of boom-and-bust repeats. Lending money on luck, borrowing against a good run, or treating a temporary upswing as permanent are the patterns to watch. This is not financial advice. The card is a reminder that the only money strategy that survives the wheel is one that assumes it will turn.

## Yes or No

Wheel of Fortune leans yes. It is a card of favorable change, and for questions about whether circumstances will improve, whether an opportunity will arrive, or whether a stuck situation will move, the answer is yes. The yes has one important qualifier: it depends on timing and events, not on your effort, so it is less reliable for questions about things you control. If you are asking "will this happen soon," the Wheel says the turn is already underway. Reversed, the lean drops to no, at least for now, with the note that no is a phase and not a verdict.

## What not to ask Wheel of Fortune

Do not ask the Wheel for a guarantee. It is the card of change, and a guarantee is the one thing it cannot give. Questions like "will this last forever" get an honest answer from this card, which is no, and that answer applies equally to good and bad situations.

Do not ask it to tell you what to do. The Wheel describes what is happening to you, not what you should make happen. If you want a plan, The Magician or The Emperor will serve you better. Ask the Wheel instead where you are in the cycle and what you learned the last time it turned.
